# Archival contact priorities

_Status: working lead map for the manufacturing/provenance lane._

This file ranks people and organizations by their likelihood of possessing **production artifacts**, not by general importance to INSIDE or the ARG.

Do not contact a large blast list. The preferred strategy is a short chain of artifact-oriented questions that can route us to the correct production owner.

## Priority B — packaging / assembly vendor

Identity: **unresolved**.

This is probably the highest-value unknown organization.

Evidence needed to identify it:

- box underside / packaging marks;
- shipping carton or fulfillment labels;
- customs/manufacturer paperwork;
- behind-the-scenes footage frames;
- iam8bit production records;
- owner invoices or replacement-shipment correspondence.

Once identified, ask for:

- job number;
- BOM;
- label/sticker line item;
- proof PDF;
- imposition sheet;
- variable-data source;
- quantity printed;
- assembly instruction.

## Priority C — RealDoll

Why lower priority:

Public evidence specifically ties RealDoll to the silicone Huddle fabrication. No public source found so far connects RealDoll to the coded sticker, printed art, or final CE packaging.

Useful binary question:

> When the Huddle left your facility, was it already inside/fixed to the final Collector's Edition packaging with the numbered symbol/image sticker present, or did iam8bit/another vendor add that later?

A negative answer cleanly removes RealDoll from the sticker production chain.

## Contact sequence

Recommended sequence:

1. iam8bit principal: identify internal owner and outside packaging/label vendor.
2. Routed iam8bit production person: ask for surviving source/proof/vendor records.
3. Playdead producer/creative lead: establish whether the sticker system was supplied as an authored puzzle asset.
4. Packaging/label vendor: request job metadata, proofs, imposition, and quantity.
5. RealDoll only to bound where final assembly happened.

## What counts as a high-value answer

The following are materially useful even without a source file:

- "Playdead supplied us a finished sheet/PDF."
- "iam8bit generated these from a spreadsheet."
- "Vendor X printed and sequentially numbered them."
- "They were applied at our warehouse after all other components arrived."
- "We printed N labels but used only M."
- "The serials were randomized / sequential / selected from a larger range."
- "There was a proof sheet with all variants."
- "The file still exists under job/project name Y."

Any of those statements would collapse large parts of the provenance search tree.

## What not to ask first

Avoid leading with:

- the entire solved mechanical model;
- claims that the code is definitely a 108-state machine;
- requests for "the answer";
- giant attachments;
- speculation about plaintext.

The first job is provenance. A short factual question is more likely to reach the person who remembers the production file.
