# Experiment 324 — Expected-player-information audit

_Status: completed initial R6 audit, 30 Sep 2026._

## Question

What could Playdead reasonably expect different classes of solver to possess?

R6 separates three things that are easy to blur together:

1. what **one Collector's Edition owner** physically receives;
2. what a **pooled contemporary community** could assemble;
3. what **later archivists** can reconstruct from years of accumulated evidence.

This matters because a plausible intended solve should begin from information a real solver could actually have, not from the present repository's reconstructed global object.

## One owner

A single Collector's Edition gives one serialized sticker fragment.

That owner can directly inspect:

- one serial number;
- one foreground mark;
- one faint background image fragment;
- that sticker's physical placement/orientation on the package.

One owner cannot establish from that object alone:

- the nine A-I background classes;
- the `IAB/CDE/FGH` assembly;
- the foreground period 108;
- the 81/27 alphabet split;
- a 9+3 per-class trace;
- repeated-residue agreement.

So the sticker puzzle is **communal by construction** if it is meant to progress beyond one fragment.

That is a strong design fact.

## Early pooled community: the background proves the model

By February-April 2020, pooled stickers were enough to establish the period-9 A-I carrier, serial phase / zero registration, the physical `IAB/CDE/FGH` assembly, and the background-layer path:

```
dat/534brn9653f9j8mmd
```

This is the crucial precedent on the same physical artifact.

Playdead demonstrably expected multiple owners' sticker fragments to be combined, spatially registered, and interpreted as one larger object.

But this successful pooled solve did **not** require anything resembling all ~600 serials.

## Foreground chronology: availability is not recognition

The foreground needs a stricter chronology distinction.

The pre-2021 public corpus was retrospectively sufficient to recover H108 under the later period search.

That does not mean solvers had explicitly recognized H108 at the moment the evidence first became sufficient.

The current chronology anchors the earliest explicit community statement found that the minimum repeat is 108 to **25 March 2021**, with more formal period/rendering work later in 2021.

This distinction is mandatory for R6:

> evidence can exist before the operation needed to recognize it is known.

## What pooled solvers gradually gained

By the 2021-2023 period, the community had independently explored or recognized:

- 108 / 12x9 organization;
- twelve 3x3 foreground squares;
- the 81/27 slash-dash versus slash-dot asymmetry;
- the tail as a possible visual aid, barrier, or metadata channel.

By 22 May 2026, a pre-machine discussion explicitly proposed a 9+3 architecture where the last three symbols act as a second selector/index into information represented by the first nine.

These are meaningful human-discovery milestones.

They still do not supply:

- primary exceptional-position ternary coding;
- G5 address/depth substitution;
- G6 selector-conditioned q readout;
- route semantics;
- terminal `100`.

## What the current archive possesses

As of this audit the canonical physical ledger contains:

- **82 physical sticker records**;
- **65 distinct H108 residues**;
- therefore **43 H108 residues with no physical observation**.

Even the observation-only one-slash tail architecture still has **36** global completions.

The incumbent preferred physical gauge has **14** complete masters because structural grammar constrains the missing cells.

That is the central R6 fact:

> the present machine is not sharp because the community physically collected nearly the whole 108-cell master. It is sharp because it imposes a highly constraining grammar on an incomplete physical corpus.

## Did Playdead expect all ~600 stickers?

There is no evidence for that.

The foreground is periodic on H108, so hundreds of serials beyond 108 mostly repeat structural positions.

The solved background layer also demonstrates that the intended communal mechanism can work from a relatively small cross-copy variant set.

Therefore “collect nearly every numbered copy” should not be treated as the default design assumption.

## Did Playdead expect all 108 foreground positions?

That is also unsupported.

The surviving 65-residue corpus is enough to expose substantial structure, while historical Playdead puzzles often supplied an ordering, registration, selector, or transform cue that reduced the need for brute-force completeness.

A more plausible design prior is:

> collect enough independent fragments to reveal the carrier, then use a sticker-native or cross-ARG cue to organize or operate on them.

That remains a prior, not a solved foreground mechanism.

## Consequence for the reset

R6 argues against research strategies whose first meaningful operation requires reconstructing a nearly complete hidden 108-cell or ~600-sticker object.

It favors hypotheses that explain how a **modest pooled sample plus a visible cue** could become solvable.

This does not invalidate the incumbent. It raises the burden on G5:

- why would contemporary solvers know to treat the tail index as a depth/address substitution;
- what cue makes that operation discoverable before its downstream success is known?

## R6 disposition

The initial expected-player-information audit is complete.

The project should henceforth distinguish:

- **single-owner facts**;
- **pooled contemporary facts**;
- **retrospectively recoverable facts**;
- **later archive-only facts**;
- **model-derived completions**.
