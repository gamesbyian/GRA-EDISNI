# Experiment 409 — reversible cover clue provenance audit

_Status: completed source/history audit, 4 Oct 2026._

The reversible PS4 slip cover has remained the highest-priority external artifact because iam8bit explicitly advertised it as containing a hidden clue.

Before using it as a consumer for the sticker foreground, this experiment asks a more basic question:

> what did the cover appear to be clueing historically, before the current machine existed?

## Source quality

The archived high-resolution A/B source pair is recoverable and was rendered through the temporary cover-source audit.

Both originals are:

```
6552 × 5040
```

The sources are therefore good enough to distinguish gross scene structure, monitor imagery, front/back composition and obvious repeated-layout features. They do not reveal an obvious nine-slot input interface, 3×3 keypad, 3×3×3 carrier, numeric table, or other direct consumer grammar for the current foreground outputs.

## Historical interpretation existed long before the current machine

The archived community discussion contains a surprisingly persistent reading of the cover.

On **27 Apr 2020**, a solver explicitly wrote that:

- the cover had two screens showing a **planet** and a **nut**;
- the planet image was not yet the final message-bearing form;
- the nut/acorn image appeared only partly;
- the covers might therefore be guides to how already-known ARG material should be read in layers or parts.

On **19 Aug 2022**, another solver made the relationship much more specific:

- the most noticeable cover clue was a monitor showing the **top half of the acorn**;
- the lower "running man" portion was absent from that monitor;
- elsewhere in the cover scene, the boy was looking toward an **actual running man**.

On **24 Dec 2022**, the community returned to the same idea. One solver called the acorn being cut off at the running-man boundary the only clue they could see; another noted that the acorn puzzle had been unsolved when the CE appeared and suggested the cover clue may therefore have become moot once that puzzle was solved.

This is not a retrospective pattern invented from the current sticker machine. It is preserved pre-machine community interpretation.

## What this supports

The cover has good evidence as a **cross-puzzle pointer/metaclue**.

In particular it plausibly teaches or reinforces operation classes such as:

- recognize an already-known puzzle image inside another scene;
- separate a composite image into meaningful components;
- attend to a partial/layer-specific region rather than the whole picture;
- treat packaging art as an instruction about another ARG artifact.

That is consistent with the broader Playdead design grammar already documented in the mechanics corpus.

## What it does not currently support

The high-resolution source pair does not independently expose a typed interface for:

- the six one-shot G5 states;
- the E2 pair;
- the stable nine-symbol Experiment-398 signature;
- the 9+3 numeric surface;
- recursive terminal `100`;
- the 84/102 unresolved bit.

There is no cover-native reason yet to overlay one of those objects onto the artwork.

## Consequence

The cover should be **downgraded as a direct sticker-foreground consumer** and **upgraded as historical operation-level provenance**.

The most economical interpretation of the advertised hidden clue is now:

> it likely points solvers toward an earlier puzzle relationship, especially the acorn/running-man decomposition, rather than serving as a nine-value endpoint for the later CE foreground code.

This remains a candidate interpretation, not an author-confirmed solution.

## Reopening trigger

Reopen direct cover consumption only if a source image or historical statement supplies one of the missing typed parameters:

- exact nine positions;
- explicit ternary labels;
- a sticker-specific alignment;
- a numbered path/traversal;
- a direct correspondence to A-I classes;
- or another independently defined consumer grammar.

Do not resume arbitrary cover overlays simply because the artifact is known to contain a clue.
