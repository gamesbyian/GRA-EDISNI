# Visual completion and external-overlay risk

_Status: operating note, 2 Oct 2026._

## Why this note exists

A current community solver made a useful methodological point after the row-order discussion:

- even a best-effort AI completion of missing sticker cells can be wrong in ways that look visually plausible;
- more importantly, an intended intermediate may contain a deliberately non-semantic or apparently random layer whose role is only revealed when combined with another artifact, overlay, seed, consumer, or transform;
- therefore a visually coherent reconstruction is not a reliable stopping criterion and a visually incoherent reconstruction is not necessarily evidence against the underlying arrangement.

This is consistent with the solved INSIDE ARG printer lineage. The PC/PS4 rows form an acorn/41 image before a later operation; the Xbox rows form a circle whose overlaid Braille payload only becomes legible after the correct registration and rotation; other printer-derived phrases are useful because they are consumed by the Playdead site and unlock subsequent artifacts.

The exact historical role of every colour/pixel layer should remain provenance-anchored. The methodological lesson does not depend on asserting that any particular sticker layer repeats the acorn pipeline.

## Consequence for the sticker code

There are now two separate uncertainties:

1. **missing physical data:** 43 H108 residues remain unobserved physically;
2. **consumer uncertainty:** even a fully reconstructed H108 surface may still require an independently supplied operation, overlay, registration, or external endpoint.

AI completion can reduce the first uncertainty only by hypothesis. It cannot remove it empirically, and it can make the second uncertainty worse by optimizing toward human-recognizable imagery that the author never intended at that stage.

Therefore:

- do not select unknown sticker fills because they make an image, glyph, word, QR-like block, Pigpen-like object, or other semantic form look cleaner;
- do not rank row permutations by image recognizability unless the image statistic was independently specified first;
- do not treat lack of visible semantic content as a falsification of an otherwise independently licensed arrangement;
- do not use generative-image completion as evidence;
- preserve model completions only as explicit predictions to be tested against newly recovered physical stickers;
- prioritize physical sticker recovery and independently cued consumer/overlay artifacts over further unconstrained visual reconstruction.

## Acquisition implication

The highest-value external evidence is now:

1. genuinely new sticker serials/residues, especially those that distinguish live completion families or hidden-state/gauge choices;
2. historical photographs or video frames that recover previously lost sticker data;
3. CE/ARG artifacts that independently specify registration, overlay, row order, seed, selector, or consumer behavior;
4. exact historical discussion showing how solvers were expected to discover such a second-stage operation.

A complete physical H108 cycle would remove the largest avoidable ambiguity. Short of that, every newly observed residue should be treated as prospective evidence against the frozen prediction matrix, not as a cell to be assimilated after the fact.

## Relationship to Experiments 360–364

- Experiment 360 shows continuity-optimized 9x12/12x9 row orders do not improve held-out sticker prediction.
- Experiment 362 shows the genuine PC acorn order has a strong continuity signal.
- Experiment 363 shows maximizing that signal nevertheless finds smoother wrong PC orders.
- Experiment 364 reconstructs the historical sticker mosaic exactly and finds that local image-like continuity still does not predict missing cells.

Together these results close the idea that better optimization alone can substitute for the missing registration/consumer clue.

The active stance is therefore:

> collect physical truth where possible; otherwise require the next operation to be supplied independently by the artifact or history.
